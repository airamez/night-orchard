Instructions

How to play

A cooperative maze for 2 to 8 players. You win together or lose together.

The goal

Your team is locked in a hedge maze at night. To win, every player walks out through the Gate onto the Outer Lawn before the end of round 16. If dawn comes first, everyone loses.

The Gate lets you through when both of these are already true:

  • Your lantern is lit. Each player lights their own, and only at one shrine.
  • The password has been spoken. It is 4 words, carved in the Orchard, and it changes every game. One player speaks it at the Gate. That opens the Gate for the whole team.

If either is missing, you stay at the Gate and that round is spent. The Event log says why.

The jobs, in an order that works

Lanterns, the key, and the hedge can happen in parallel. The Gate is past the hedge, and the password is past a locked door, so those two come first.

  1. Read the Event log and agree a plan in team chat.
  2. Carry the iron key from Bell Lawn to the Glass House and unlock the east door.
  3. Read the 4 words in the Orchard and say them in chat, in order.
  4. Light every lantern at its own shrine. Watch the groundskeeper on the way.
  5. Cross the woven hedge into the Rose Walk. Someone has to hold it open that round.
  6. Speak the password at the Gate. Then each player with a lit lantern walks north onto the Outer Lawn.

Where you start

One person presses Create room and becomes the host. Everyone else joins with the link or the four-letter code. When at least two people are in, the host presses Start. The room holds 8. Names are at most 14 characters, so they fit on the map.

Join before the host starts. After that, the team is set for the game.

Join order gives you a starting room and a lantern. The shrine that lights your lantern is often a different room. The lantern line on your screen names that shrine.

Your screen

Your room panel shows the room you stand in: its exits, its clues, who is with you, and whether the iron key is here. The clues you need are scattered, so the team explores together.

The Event log shares those clues. It posts every starting room at the beginning. The first time anyone enters a new room, its text appears for everyone, with that player’s name and the round. Each room is posted once per game.

Use team chat for the plan: who takes the key, who lights a lantern, and who holds the hedge.

Example

Artur starts in the Fountain Court. The Event log shares its clue: “The big key is under the bell.” Airamez, on the Bell Lawn, reads it and writes in chat: “I’ll pick up the key and take it to the Glass House.”

The maze is fixed. Every game uses the same 13 rooms, paths, shrines, and groundskeeper patrol. During play, the team map reveals rooms as anyone visits them. A connected room you have not entered yet shows its real name in gray. Green paths are ordinary walks. The solid red path between Hedge Knot and Rose Walk needs a holder. The dashed red path between Glass House and the Orchard needs the iron key.

How a round works

  1. Choose one action. The buttons are the choices for the room you are standing in.
  2. Press Lock action. Press Locked — change until everyone has locked.
  3. When the last player locks, the round happens for everyone at once. Then the groundskeeper takes one step.

There are only 16 rounds, so a spent round is gone. Players already on the Outer Lawn still lock each round. Wait keeps them outside. A seat that never locks stops the round. The host can press Set Wait for someone still choosing, or Kick a seat that has stalled.

If you are delayed, the action you lock this round is cancelled. The log says you stumble, and the delay then clears.

The iron key

One iron key starts on Bell Lawn, in the grass under the bell. Fountain Court says the same thing, and that line is in the Event log from round 1.

  • Pick up the iron key while you stand in its room. One player carries it. If two players pick it up in the same round, the one who joined earlier gets it.
  • Drop the iron key leaves it in your current room.
  • Use the iron key on the east door, in the Glass House, opens the Orchard. The key is used up. The door stays open, and East to The Orchard appears.

Each of those is a whole round, and so is each step of the walk.

Example

From Bell Lawn the short carry is west to the Hedge Knot, south to Fountain Court, south to the Well, and east to the Glass House. That is four walks, plus one round to pick the key up and one round to use it. The player who started at the Well is already closer to the door.

The password

The Orchard is through the Glass House’s east door. The bark there names 4 words and the order to say them. A new game, and every restart, carves a different set.

At the Gate, type the words in that order, with spaces between them, and press Speak. Capitals and extra spaces are ignored. The order is the password.

A correct speak opens the Gate for the rest of the game. A wrong speak spends the round, and the Gate stays silent. Say the words in chat as soon as someone reads the carving. The plaque on the Gate sends you to the Orchard for the answer.

Your lantern

Stand in your shrine and press Light your … lantern. The button is offered there while your lantern is still dark. A lit lantern stays lit.

  • Oak wakes at Oak Shrine.
  • Well wakes at The Well.
  • Bell wakes at Bell Lawn.
  • Moon wakes at Moon Arch.

The Gate turns an unlit lantern back, even after the password has been spoken.

The woven hedge

When you stand in the Hedge Knot, your room text reads:

Example

Hedge Knot
Four hedges meet. The north one is woven shut.
A sign reads:
It opens while someone holds the sun stone, or holds the walk beyond it.

  • Four hedges meet. South to Fountain Court, west to the Sun Stone, east to Bell Lawn, and north to the Rose Walk.
  • The north one is woven shut. Choosing North while it is shut spends the round where you stand. The button is marked (shut hedge).
  • Someone holds the sun stone. A player on the Sun Stone chooses Hold that round.
  • Or holds the walk beyond it. A player already on the Rose Walk can choose Hold instead.
  • It opens for that round only. Every crossing needs a fresh Hold.
  • Hold is the holder’s whole action. They stay where they are.
  • Hold is offered on the Sun Stone and the Rose Walk. The first crossing needs someone on the Sun Stone. After that, a player already on the Rose Walk can hold for the next teammate.

Rose Walk connects to the Hedge Knot and to the Gate. This hedge is the only road to the Gate, and every player walks through the Gate to escape.

Example

Getting two players through. Round 5: Jose stands on the Sun Stone and locks Hold; Leila at the Hedge Knot locks North and crosses to the Rose Walk. Round 6: Leila locks Hold; Jose walks east from the Sun Stone to the Hedge Knot. Round 7: Leila locks Hold again; Jose locks North and crosses too. Leila can then walk north toward the Gate while Jose stays on the Rose Walk, holding for the next teammate.

Example

A wasted round. Artur is alone at the Hedge Knot and locks North. Nobody holds this round, so the hedge stays shut. The fix was agreed one round earlier in chat: “I need someone on the Sun Stone next round.”

The groundskeeper

He walks a fixed patrol, and you can see it on the map. He starts in the Oak Shrine: Oak Shrine → Bell Lawn → Hedge Knot → Fountain Court → Hedge Knot → Bell Lawn, and around again.

If you end a round in the room he walks into, you are delayed: your next locked action is cancelled. A teammate in that room can choose Distract the groundskeeper. They spend their round, and nobody who ends there is delayed. The Distract button appears only in the room he is about to enter, on that round.

Ivy Wall is off his patrol. So are the Sun Stone, the Rose Walk, the Gate, the Outer Lawn, and the rooms south of Fountain Court.

Example

The groundskeeper is heading to the Fountain Court, where Artur needs to stay one more round. He locks Distract the groundskeeper. The groundskeeper arrives, Artur keeps him talking, and nobody is delayed. Artur gave up his whole round to do it.

Walking out

From the Gate, with your lantern lit and the password already spoken, choose North to Outer Lawn. The game ends when the last player steps onto the lawn. People who got out early should stay in chat, and keep locking Wait so the round can finish. Walking south from the lawn puts you back inside the Gate.

  • Plan the next round in chat before you lock. You can still change a lock until everyone has locked.
  • Count the groundskeeper’s steps. His route repeats, so his next room is always knowable.
  • Holding and distracting spend the round in place. They are often the move that lets someone else escape. The end screen remembers who helped.
  • Spread out. The first visit to a room posts its clues for the whole team.
  • The host can Restart game. Everyone returns to the starting rooms at round 1, and a new password is drawn. Team chat and the players stay.

When the game ends, every screen changes to the same end screen, win or lose.
